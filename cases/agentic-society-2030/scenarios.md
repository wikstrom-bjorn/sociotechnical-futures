# Scenarios — 2030

Three branches built on the uncertainties in [uncertainties.md](uncertainties.md), primarily #1
(regulatory trajectory) and #3 (does agency loss surface publicly).

## A. Delegated Convenience (continuation of current trajectory)

Regulation stays at the disclosure layer; no single trigger event makes agency loss legible; agent
infrastructure consolidates around 3–4 "agent of record" platforms. By 2030, agentic delegation is a
normalized default for routine consumption and scheduling decisions, the way autofill and
recommendation engines became normalized in the 2010s. Formal metrics (satisfaction, efficiency,
transaction volume) look good. The Perceptual Legitimacy Gap widens quietly: people report high
satisfaction with individual transactions and, separately and without connecting the two, declining
trust that institutions and markets serve their interests. This is the base case if nothing in
[drivers.md](drivers.md) changes direction.

## B. Agentic Backlash (trigger event + fast regulatory response)

A high-visibility harm event — a financial, health, or legal decision made autonomously by an agent
with contested or damaging consequences at scale — makes the agency-loss mechanism suddenly legible.
Public reaction moves faster than in scenario A; regulation shifts from disclosure to mandatory
active confirmation for high-stakes decision categories (finance, health, housing, legal). Consumer
trust in agentic commerce drops sharply in the affected category and recovers slowly, similar to
historical trust shocks in other automated-decision domains. Agent providers compete on "verifiable
restraint" (what an agent will *not* do without explicit confirmation) rather than pure capability.

## C. Governed Agency (institutional adaptation without a crisis trigger)

Neither a‑style drift nor a b‑style shock: instead, the Article 50 disclosure regime becomes the seed
of a broader legibility infrastructure — standardized, auditable "agency logs" showing what an agent
decided and why, adopted proactively by major platforms ahead of stricter regulation, partly to avoid
scenario B. Delegation continues to expand, but with designed checkpoints that preserve legible,
recoverable friction at specific decision thresholds (see [opportunities.md](opportunities.md)) rather
than removing it uniformly. This is the scenario in which the mechanism identified in
[mechanisms.md](mechanisms.md) is treated as a design constraint from the start, rather than
discovered after the fact.

## Reading these scenarios

A is not neutral — it is what happens if the palliation mechanism in [mechanisms.md](mechanisms.md)
is correct and nothing intervenes. B and C are not opposites of A so much as two different ways an
intervention could occur: reactively (B, after a legible harm) or proactively (C, by design). The
opportunities in [opportunities.md](opportunities.md) are written to make C more likely without
requiring B to happen first.
