# Opportunities

What organizations can actually build, design, or decide differently, given the mechanism in
[mechanisms.md](mechanisms.md) — not just the symptom in [signals.md](signals.md).

## Design legible friction back in, deliberately, at specific thresholds

The intervention implied by [mechanisms.md](mechanisms.md) is not "add more friction everywhere" —
that simply forfeits the real convenience gains documented in [signals.md](signals.md). It is to
identify the specific decision thresholds (spend amount, reversibility, category sensitivity — health,
housing, finance) where preserving an active, legible checkpoint protects felt agency without
sacrificing the efficiency case for delegation elsewhere. This is a concrete design brief, not a
general caution.

## Build "agency logs," not just disclosure labels

Article 50 requires disclosure that an agent is acting and on whose behalf (see [signals.md](
signals.md)). That is a compliance floor, not a trust asset. An auditable, user-facing record of what
an agent decided, on what basis, and what it did *not* do without confirmation, converts a regulatory
requirement into the "verifiable restraint" advantage described in scenario C
([scenarios.md](scenarios.md)) — and is buildable now, ahead of any stricter mandate.

## Treat the accountability gap as a product decision, not just a legal one

[Uncertainties.md](uncertainties.md) #5 (who is accountable when a delegated decision goes wrong) will
be resolved by regulation eventually either way. Organizations that design a clear, user-facing answer
to that question *before* being forced to — rather than defaulting to terms-of-service disclaimers —
are the ones positioned to avoid becoming the trigger event in scenario B.

## Measure experiential legitimacy, not just satisfaction

The core empirical risk in scenario A is that every individual metric looks fine while the aggregate
relationship erodes. Organizations serious about avoiding that outcome need a measurement layer beyond
transaction-level CSAT — something closer to the Perceptual Legitimacy Gap construct itself: does the
user still feel the outcome was theirs to decide, tracked over time and in aggregate, not just per
transaction.
