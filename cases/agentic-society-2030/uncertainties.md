# Uncertainties

The variables that are genuinely open, and that the scenarios in [scenarios.md](scenarios.md) branch
on.

## 1. Does regulation move from disclosure to substantive limits on delegation?

Article 50 (2026) regulates legibility, not delegation scope. Whether the EU (or other jurisdictions)
moves toward limiting *what* can be delegated without active per-decision confirmation — especially
for financial, health, or legal decisions — is unresolved and depends on political response to
whichever incidents surface first.

## 2. Does agent infrastructure consolidate or stay contestable?

UCP/AP2/A2A/MCP could mature into genuinely open, multi-vendor infrastructure (like email or the web)
or could become a small number of closed "agent of record" relationships controlled by whichever
platform wins the current competition (see [drivers.md](drivers.md)). This determines whether users
retain meaningful choice about which agent represents them, or whether that choice collapses to a
platform default.

## 3. Does felt agency loss surface as a nameable public issue, or stay diffuse?

AoNR's core empirical claim is that this kind of erosion is typically *not* experienced as a nameable
grievance — it shows up as withdrawal, apathy, or distrust without a clear cause. Whether agentic
delegation produces a *visible* trigger event (a high-profile harmful autonomous purchase or decision)
that makes the mechanism legible to the public — versus staying diffuse the way palliation typically
does — materially changes which scenario plays out.

## 4. Does the abandonment rate (40% of projects, per Gartner) reflect a genuine capability ceiling or an implementation-maturity problem?

If agentic AI's current failure modes (unclear ROI, data quality, cost, see [signals.md](signals.md))
are solved within 1–2 years, adoption likely accelerates past the current cautious-piloting phase. If
they are closer to structural (agents perform worse than expected on judgment-heavy tasks at scale),
adoption may plateau well short of the "agent handles most decisions" scenarios.

## 5. Who is held accountable when a delegated decision goes wrong?

Liability frameworks for agent-caused harm remain largely unsettled across jurisdictions as of this
case. Where that liability lands — user, platform, agent developer, or an unresolved gap — shapes both
regulatory trajectory (#1) and public trust dynamics (#3).
