# Drivers

The structural forces producing the signals in [signals.md](signals.md).

## Economic

- **Cost pressure on service/decision labour.** The measured wins where agentic AI *has* scaled
  (customer service, contract review, hiring timelines) are all cost-per-transaction reductions in
  high-volume decision work — the same economic logic that has driven service-design and UX practice
  toward frictionlessness for the past two decades, now applied to cognitive/decision labour rather
  than interface labour.
- **Platform competition for the "agent of record."** Google, Amazon, OpenAI, Meta, and Perplexity are
  not competing on agent capability alone — they are competing to be the default agent a person
  delegates to, because whoever holds that position captures the transaction layer of an entire
  category of decisions. This is a platform-economics driver, not a technology-readiness driver.

## Technical / infrastructural

- **Interoperability standards matured faster than most institutions adapted to them.** UCP, AP2,
  A2A, and MCP turn "an AI agent can act on your behalf" from a single-vendor feature into shared
  infrastructure — which is what converts a capability into a default, adopted-without-deciding layer.

## Institutional / regulatory

- **Regulatory response lags capability and targets legibility, not delegation itself.** The EU's
  Article 50 response is a disclosure requirement, not a limit on what agents may do. That is a
  rational institutional response *given* the mechanism in [mechanisms.md](mechanisms.md): regulators
  are moving to restore legibility (can the person tell they're dealing with an agent?) rather than to
  slow delegation, because delegation itself has not yet produced a legible harm to regulate against.

## Cultural

- **A pre-existing convenience norm.** Two decades of service design, UX research, and "reduce
  friction" as an unquestioned design maxim (the author's own earlier professional practice, see
  [base76-research](https://github.com/wikstrom-bjorn/base76-research)) built the cultural expectation
  that any point of friction in a transaction is a defect to be removed. Agentic AI is the logical
  next iteration of that norm, not a break from it — it removes the last remaining friction point:
  the decision itself.
