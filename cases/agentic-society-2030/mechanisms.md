# Mechanisms

*This is the step most trend reports skip. It is also where this case draws directly on the author's
published theoretical work — see [base76-research](https://github.com/wikstrom-bjorn/base76-research)
— rather than treating "AI agents" as a self-contained new phenomenon.*

## Primary mechanism: palliation extended from interface to decision

*The Age of No Resistance* (Wikström, 2026, *Acta Sociologica*) theorizes palliation: rationalized
systems reduce friction at the points of contact where people previously experienced agency,
recognition, and integration — and formal legitimacy (efficiency, outcomes, compliance) can hold or
improve even as *experiential* legitimacy erodes, because the removed friction was also where felt
agency lived.

Agentic AI is the same mechanism operating one layer deeper. Service design and digital platforms
already removed friction from *how* a decision gets executed (fewer clicks, fewer forms, fewer steps).
Agentic delegation removes friction from *whether a decision gets made by the person at all*. The
mechanism does not change; the layer it operates on does.

## Secondary mechanism: cognitive subsumption at the point of delegation

The author's *Cognitive Subsumption* thesis (under review, *AI & SOCIETY*) — capital and the
enclosure of the deliberative ground — describes how the space in which deliberation happens gets
progressively absorbed into systems the individual does not control. Agentic delegation is a direct
instance: the deliberative ground for "should I buy this, at this price, from this seller" moves from
the person's own reasoning into an agent's optimization function, set by a platform whose incentives
(capturing the transaction layer, see [drivers.md](drivers.md)) are not the same as the user's.

## Why this produces a legitimacy gap, not just a convenience gain

The mechanism predicts a specific, falsifiable pattern: agentic delegation will be experienced as
*convenient* and *individually rational* to adopt (which is exactly what the 58%-trust and
20%-AI-influenced-orders signals show), while simultaneously producing a slow, hard-to-attribute
erosion in people's sense that decisions affecting them are actually theirs — visible not as immediate
backlash but as the same kind of diffuse, hard-to-name distance from institutions that AoNR documents
for welfare-state services. The EU's move to mandate disclosure (Article 50) is a leading institutional
symptom of exactly this: legibility, not capability, becomes the site of regulatory anxiety.

## What would falsify this

If longitudinal data (2027–2030) shows delegation correlating with *stable or increased* felt agency
and institutional trust — not just satisfaction with individual transactions — the palliation/
subsumption mechanism would need revision. This is the empirical claim [uncertainties.md](
uncertainties.md) and [scenarios.md](scenarios.md) are built to track.
